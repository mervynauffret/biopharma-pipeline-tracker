How I'd use this in a PMO

Weekly status

The KPI strip and flag board are the one-page status report: what changed, what is due and what needs chasing, refreshed every Monday without manual work.

Stage-gate preparation

The heatmap and timeline show where the portfolio sits and which readouts feed the next go/no-go decisions, so committee packs start from the same picture.

Risk and action log

Past-target and unverified trials become open questions with an owner. The slip tracker turns date changes into variance against a baseline, the way a programme schedule is controlled.

How this is built

  • Source: ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, trials where the company is lead sponsor and the status is recruiting, active, not yet recruiting or enrolling by invitation.
  • Snapshot date: every rule compares against the date the data was pulled, not the date you are reading. A month-only date (e.g. "Sep 2026") counts as the last day of that month.
  • Past target: primary completion date is marked estimated by the sponsor and is earlier than the snapshot date. This can be a real delay or a registry entry that hasn't been updated: both are worth a question.
  • Not verified 12m+: the sponsor hasn't confirmed the record in over a year. Due ≤6 months: estimated primary completion within six months, Phase 2 or later. Data in: the primary completion date is actual, so the trial is in follow-up. On track: none of the above.
  • Big pharma benchmark: large companies, Phase 3 and 2/3 trials only, kept in their own view so they don't swamp the mid-size biotech picture.
  • Refresh: a scheduled job pulls a new snapshot every Monday and keeps each week's file, so date slips build up as history.
  • Caveats: registry data can lag reality, and sponsors update at different speeds. Galapagos was in scope but returned no trials under that sponsor name. Public registry data only; not investment advice.
